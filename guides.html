01

Choose your workflow first

A semi-automatic setup asks you to grind, dose, tamp, start the shot, and steam milk yourself. That routine is part of the appeal if you want to learn. It also means your grinder and technique matter.

A super-automatic machine grinds and brews at a button press; models with an automatic milk system handle that too. It reduces the steps and the learning curve, but gives you fewer ways to control each shot.

02

Set a full setup budget

For semi-automatic espresso, don’t allocate the entire budget to the machine. A capable grinder is a core part of the setup, not a later luxury. Start with the total you want to spend on the main gear and shop as a pair.

Around $500Compact machine + espresso-focused grinder

A sensible entry point for learning with a separate grinder. It leaves little room for add-ons.

Up to $800More choice in milk workflow or machine style

Compare manual steaming, automatic milk texturing, and bean-to-cup convenience.

Over $800Only pay more for a feature you’ll use

Spend extra on a clearer need—such as easier milk drinks, more brew control, or a grinder for multiple methods.

These are planning bands based on manufacturer list pricing when checked, before tax and accessories. Retail pricing moves; confirm the total on the day you buy.

03

Match the machine to the grinder

Espresso needs fine grind adjustments. With a semi-auto machine, choose a grinder explicitly designed to cover espresso rather than assuming any burr grinder will work. Our short list starts with the Baratza Encore ESP; the Fellow Opus 2 is worth comparing if you also switch between espresso and brewed coffee.

04

Leave room for the small stuff

Plan for fresh beans, a scale that reads to tenths of a gram, and a suitable way to store coffee. Some machines include a basic tamper and milk pitcher; check what’s in the exact box before buying accessories. A water filter or descaler may also matter depending on your water and the maker’s care instructions.

Buy only what helps you use the machine you chose. A neat starter setup is better than a drawer of gear you haven’t needed.
